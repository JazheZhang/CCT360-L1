My first scroll story

My first dog was Sunny

I had never had a dog of my own before him. Every walk in these photos is still, somehow, a little bit of a first.

Week one

The dog I did not know how to have

I grew up around other people's dogs, never my own. So the first night I did not know whether he was allowed on the bed, whether the whining meant something was wrong, whether I was doing any of it right. Sunny did not seem to notice the difference. He picked a spot by the door and fell asleep like he had always lived there.

The first year

Learning together

Neither of us knew what we were doing, which somehow made it easier. I learned what his bark before the mail carrier sounds like versus his bark at nothing at all. He learned my footsteps on the stairs, the exact click of my door before a walk. Every mistake I made teaching him something was really the two of us figuring it out at the same time.

Years since

The one who never needed convincing

Whatever else was happening that year, he was the one constant that asked nothing complicated of me. Not "how was your day" in the way that expects a real answer, just a body at the door, glad, every single time, like it was the first time. I had never had that from an animal before him.

Now

This walk, this light

This is one of those ordinary walks, years into having him, that I still stopped to photograph. Low sun catching every hair on his back, tongue out, entirely unbothered by how good he looks. He has been my dog for long enough now that it is strange to remember a version of my life before him, but this photo is proof there was one.

Tomorrow

The first, and the favorite

People say you never forget your first dog. I believe them, though I did not understand it until I had one. Whatever dogs come after him, Sunny gets to be the one who taught me how any of this works, and the one still waiting by the door tomorrow.
